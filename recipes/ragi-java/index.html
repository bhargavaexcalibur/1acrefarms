Ragi Java (Sweet Finger Millet Porridge)

రాగి జావ · रागी माल्ट · रागि-यवागूः · Ragi Java · Ragi Malt

Ragi java is a pourable porridge of ragi flour cooked in water, a daily drink in many Andhra and Rayalaseema homes. The salted version is made with buttermilk; this sweet one is loosened with milk and sweetened with jaggery, and it is drunk warm from a steel tumbler at breakfast or in the evening.

Our Thati Bellam palm jaggery suits the earthy taste of ragi and gives the java a toffee-brown colour. It takes about 15 minutes from start to finish. This recipe serves 2.

  • Prep3 min
  • Cook12 min
  • Total17 min
  • Serves2

Total includes the 2-minute rest before the jaggery goes in.

In short

Whisk ragi flour into water, cook it in boiling water till thick and glossy, then add milk. Off the heat, stir in palm jaggery and cardamom.

Ingredients serves 2

For the java

  • ¼ cup ragi flour finger millet flour, about 35 g
  • 2 cups water 1 cup to mix the flour, 1 cup to boil
  • 1 cup milk about 250 ml

To sweeten

  • 3 tbsp grated palm jaggery about 30 g; add a spoon more if you like it sweeter
  • 1 pinch cardamom powder optional

Method

  1. Put the ragi flour in a bowl.
  2. Whisk the ragi flour into 1 cup water till smooth, with no lumps. Use a whisk or a fork and break up any lumps against the side of the bowl.
  3. Pour the other cup of water into a heavy-bottomed pan; a thin pan lets the ragi catch.
  4. Boil the other cup of water, pour in the ragi and stir on low heat till thick and glossy, 6–8 minutes. Stir the mix once more before pouring, as the flour settles at the bottom of the bowl.
  5. Keep stirring all the time. The java is cooked when it turns from dull pinkish-brown to a darker, glossy brown and coats the back of the spoon.
  6. Add the milk and simmer 2 minutes. Switch off and rest 2 minutes. Stir once or twice while it simmers so it does not catch.
  7. Grate the palm jaggery finely while the java rests, so it melts quickly.
  8. Stir in the palm jaggery and cardamom. Serve warm. Stir till the jaggery has fully melted, then taste and add a little more if needed.
  9. Pour into tumblers or bowls. If it is too thick to drink, stir in a few spoons of warm milk.

Tips

  • Jaggery goes in off the heat so the milk does not split.
  • Always mix the ragi flour in water first; flour added straight to boiling water forms lumps that do not break up.
  • Do not rush the cooking; undercooked ragi tastes raw and floury.
  • For a thinner java to drink from a tumbler, use 2 tbsp ragi flour; for a spoonable porridge, use ⅓ cup.

Variations

  • Cane jaggery: use the same amount of grated cane jaggery for a lighter, caramel-like sweetness.
  • With dry fruit: stir in a few chopped cashews or almonds and a spoon of raisins at the end.
  • Without milk: cook the ragi in 2½ cups water and stir in the jaggery once it has stopped bubbling, for a thinner, darker java.

Serving

Serve warm in a steel tumbler or bowl for breakfast, or as an evening drink with a few biscuits or murukulu. Some like it chilled in summer.

Storage

Best drunk the day it is made. Keep leftovers covered in the fridge for up to 1 day and stir in a little warm milk, off the heat, when reheating.

Questions

How do I make ragi java without lumps?

Whisk the ragi flour into room-temperature water first until smooth, then pour this into boiling water while stirring all the time.

How do I know ragi java is cooked?

It turns darker and glossy, thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon, and no longer tastes floury. This takes about 6–8 minutes on low heat.

Can I use cane jaggery in ragi java?

Yes. Use the same amount of grated cane jaggery (cheruku bellam) and stir it in after switching off the stove, so the milk does not split. It gives a lighter colour and a milder, caramel-like sweetness.

What is the difference between ragi java and ragi malt?

They are the same drink with different names. Ragi java is the Telugu name; ragi malt is what it is often called in shops and on menus.
