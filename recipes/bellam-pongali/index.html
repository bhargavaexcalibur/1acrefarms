Bellam Pongali (Jaggery Sweet Pongal)

బెల్లం పొంగలి · गुड़ वाला मीठा पोंगल · गुडमधुरं पोंगलम् · Bellam Pongali · Gud Wala Meetha Pongal

Bellam pongali is the sweet pongal that Telugu homes cook on Sankranti morning: rice and roasted moong dal cooked very soft, then sweetened with jaggery and enriched with ghee. It is offered as naivedyam before the festive meal, and it is also the prasadam at many temples and Friday pujas.

Cane jaggery gives it a light brown colour and a round, toffee-like sweetness, and ghee-fried whole cashews from our Jeedipappu pack add the crunch. This recipe serves 4.

  • Prep10 min
  • Cook35 min
  • Total45 min
  • Serves4

Cook time is for a pressure cooker; allow about 15 minutes more in a pot.

In short

Cook roasted moong dal and rice till very soft, stir in melted cane jaggery and simmer till glossy. Finish with ghee-fried cashews, raisins and cardamom.

Ingredients serves 4

For the pongali

  • ½ cup rice sona masoori or another short-grain rice, about 100 g
  • ¼ cup moong dal split yellow moong dal (pesarapappu), about 50 g
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 cup grated cane jaggery lightly packed, about 150 g; a little more if you like it sweeter
  • ¼ cup water to melt the jaggery

To finish

  • 4 tbsp ghee divided
  • 15 cashews whole or halved
  • 1 tbsp raisins
  • ½ tsp cardamom powder or the crushed seeds of 4–5 pods
  • a pinch of edible camphor (pacha karpooram) optional; a very small pinch

Method

  1. Roast the moong dal till light golden, then cook it with the rice till very soft and mushy. Start by dry-roasting the dal in a pressure cooker or heavy-bottomed pot on low heat, stirring, until it smells nutty and turns light golden, about 3–4 minutes.
  2. Add the rice to the roasted dal, wash both together in 2–3 changes of water, and drain.
  3. Add the 3 cups water. Pressure cook for 4–5 whistles on medium heat and let the pressure drop on its own, or simmer covered in a pot for 25–30 minutes, adding a splash of hot water if it dries out.
  4. Open and mash the rice and dal lightly with the back of a ladle. It should be soft and porridge-like, not dry.
  5. Melt the jaggery in ¼ cup water and pour it into the rice. Warm the jaggery and water in a small pan on low heat, stirring, just until it dissolves, about 2–3 minutes; it does not need to boil.
  6. Simmer, stirring, till thick and glossy, 5–7 minutes; stir in 2 tbsp ghee. Keep the heat low and stir all the way to the bottom, as the pongali catches easily once the jaggery is in.
  7. Taste, and stir in a little more melted jaggery if you want it sweeter.
  8. Fry the cashews in the rest of the ghee; add with the cardamom. Heat the remaining 2 tbsp ghee in a small pan on medium-low heat and stir the cashews until golden, about 1 minute, adding the raisins for the last few seconds until they puff up.
  9. Pour the cashews, raisins and ghee over the pongali, sprinkle in the cardamom powder and the camphor, if using, and stir well.
  10. Switch off and serve warm. Pongali thickens as it stands; loosen it with a few spoons of hot water if needed.

Tips

  • Cook the rice and dal fully soft; they stop softening once the jaggery goes in.
  • Roasting the moong dal gives pongali its nutty smell; keep the heat low so it turns golden, not brown.
  • Use a heavy-bottomed pan and keep stirring once the jaggery is in; it catches and burns quickly.
  • Take the pongali off the heat while it is still a little loose; it sets as it cools.

Variations

  • Palm jaggery: use the same amount of grated palm jaggery for a darker pongali with a deeper, smoky sweetness.
  • Chakkera pongali: use ¾ cup sugar in place of the jaggery for a pale, sugar-sweet version.
  • Coconut: fry 2 tbsp thin slivers of dry coconut in the ghee along with the cashews.

Serving

Offer it as naivedyam on Sankranti morning, then serve it warm with the festive meal, alongside pulihora and garelu.

Storage

Keep leftovers covered in the fridge and eat within 2 days. Warm with a splash of hot water and a little ghee, stirring, before serving.

Questions

What is the difference between pongali and chakkera pongali?

Pongali on its own often means the savoury version made with pepper and cumin. Chakkera pongali and bellam pongali are the sweet versions, made with sugar or jaggery.

Can I make bellam pongali in a pressure cooker?

Yes. Cook the roasted dal and rice with 3 cups water for 4–5 whistles, let the pressure drop on its own, then add the melted jaggery and simmer in the open cooker.

Why did my pongali turn hard after cooling?

Pongali sets as it cools, and simmering too long after the jaggery goes in makes it firmer. Take it off while it is still a little loose, and loosen leftovers with hot water.

Can I use palm jaggery for pongali?

Yes. Use the same amount of grated palm jaggery (thati bellam). The pongali turns darker and tastes deeper and slightly smoky.
