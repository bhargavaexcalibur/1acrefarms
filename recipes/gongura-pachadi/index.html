Gongura Pachadi (Andhra Sorrel Leaf Chutney)

గోంగూర పచ్చడి · गोंगुरा चटनी · गोंगूर-चटनी · Gongura Pachadi · Gongura Chutney

Gongura pachadi is the chutney Andhra is best known for: sour gongura (sorrel) leaves fried down in oil and ground with roasted spices, garlic and red chilli. Around Guntur and Palnadu it is on the table almost every day, mixed into hot rice with a spoon of ghee, with sliced onion on the side.

Teja chilli powder gives it the deep red colour and the sharp heat that stands up to the sourness of the leaves. This recipe makes about 1½ cups, enough for 6.

  • Prep15 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Total45 min
  • Serves6

Total includes cooling the leaves and spices before grinding.

In short

Fry gongura leaves in oil till they turn to a dark mash, then grind them with roasted coriander, fenugreek, garlic, Teja chilli powder and salt. Finish with a mustard and dal tempering.

Ingredients serves 6

For the pachadi

  • 4 cups gongura leaves picked from 2 large bunches, about 200 g
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil gingelly oil, for frying the leaves
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds menthulu; no more, or the pachadi turns bitter
  • 6 garlic cloves peeled
  • 2 tsp Teja chilli powder very hot; use 1 tsp for a milder pachadi
  • ¾ tsp salt or to taste

For the tempering (popu)

  • 2 tbsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 tsp chana dal
  • 1 tsp urad dal
  • 2 dried red chillies broken in half
  • 4 garlic cloves lightly crushed
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • a pinch of asafoetida (hing)

Method

  1. Pick the gongura leaves off the stems and wash them in 2–3 changes of water. Spread them on a cloth and pat them completely dry; water left on the leaves splutters in the oil and shortens how long the pachadi keeps.
  2. Roast the coriander, cumin and fenugreek till fragrant; let them cool. Use a dry pan on low heat and stir all the time: the fenugreek should turn one shade darker, about 2 minutes, but not brown.
  3. Fry the gongura leaves in 1 tbsp oil till they wilt to a dark mash, 6–8 minutes. Use a wide pan on medium heat and stir often; the leaves shrink fast, turn from green to a dull olive-brown, and almost all their water cooks off.
  4. Spread the leaves on a plate and let them cool completely, about 10 minutes.
  5. Grind the spices with the garlic, chilli powder and salt, then pulse in the leaves. Grind the roasted spices, the 6 garlic cloves, Teja chilli powder and salt to a coarse powder first.
  6. Add the cooled leaves and pulse 3–4 times to a coarse paste, without any water. Many Andhra homes pound it in a stone mortar (rolu) instead.
  7. Taste: it should be sour, hot and well salted. Add a pinch more salt if the sourness feels too sharp.
  8. Temper mustard, dals, red chillies and curry leaves in the rest of the oil; mix it in. Heat the 2 tbsp sesame oil in a small pan, let the mustard seeds crackle, fry the chana dal and urad dal till golden, then add the red chillies, crushed garlic, curry leaves and asafoetida for a few seconds. Pour it over the pachadi and stir well.
  9. Let the pachadi sit for 30 minutes if you can, so the flavours come together, then spoon it into a clean, dry glass jar.

Tips

  • Dry the leaves fully before frying so the pachadi keeps longer.
  • Teja chilli powder is very hot. For a milder pachadi use 1 tsp, or half Teja and half Sannam chilli powder.
  • Keep the fenugreek to ¼ tsp and roast it only one shade darker; more, or darker, makes the pachadi bitter.
  • Pulse, do not grind smooth: gongura pachadi should be coarse, with bits of leaf you can see.
  • Do not skimp on the oil; the layer of oil on top helps the pachadi keep.

Variations

  • With onion: stir 1 small onion, finely chopped, into a portion just before serving, and eat it the same day.
  • Whole red chillies: in place of the chilli powder, fry 12–15 dried red chillies in a little oil and grind them with the spices.
  • Green chilli gongura: fry 6–8 green chillies with the leaves and cut the chilli powder to 1 tsp, for a sharper, greener heat.

Serving

Mix a spoon into hot rice with ghee and eat with sliced raw onion on the side. It also goes well with dosa, idli and as a side on any Andhra meal plate.

Storage

Keep it in a clean, dry glass jar in the fridge for up to 2 weeks, always using a dry spoon. A thin layer of oil on top helps it keep.

Questions

What is gongura called in English?

Gongura is the leaf of the roselle plant, often sold as sorrel leaves or red sorrel. In Hindi- and Marathi-speaking areas it is called ambadi.

How do I make gongura pachadi less sour?

Add a little more salt and chilli, or stir in 1 tsp grated jaggery. Frying the leaves down well also mellows their sourness.

Why is my gongura pachadi bitter?

Usually too much fenugreek, or fenugreek roasted too dark. Keep it to ¼ tsp and stop roasting when it is one shade darker.

How long does gongura pachadi last?

About 2 weeks in the fridge in a dry glass jar, if the leaves were dried well and you always use a dry spoon. Outside the fridge, eat it within 2–3 days.
