Cashew grades explained: W180, W240, W320 and more

జీడిపప్పు · काजू · काजूतकम् · Jeedipappu · Kaju

W180, W240, W320: these are cashew kernel grades, and each letter and number means something exact. In India the grades are set out in AGMARK's Cashew Kernels Grading and Marking Rules, and the Cashew Export Promotion Council of India (CEPCI) describes the grades India exports.

This guide explains each grade name, gives the kernels per pound and per kg for the whole grades, and says what a grade tells you and what it does not. It also covers which kind suits which dish, where Andhra's cashews grow and how to keep them. There is a Telugu section further down.

In short

In a grade such as W320, W means white whole kernels and the number is how many kernels make a pound (454 g): W320 is 300 to 320 kernels a pound, or 660 to 705 a kg. A smaller number means bigger kernels, and W180 is the biggest grade listed. SW means scorched wholes, browner from longer roasting. S, B, LWP, SWP and BB are splits, butts and pieces. A grade describes size, colour and wholeness, not taste.

What the grade names mean

W: white wholes (W180, W210, W240, W320, W450 and more)
Whole kernels that are white, pale ivory or light ash in colour. The number is how many kernels make a pound (454 g), so a smaller number means bigger kernels.
SW: scorched wholes
Whole kernels that are light brown, light ivory, light ash or deep ivory in colour from scorching, which AGMARK puts down to overheating and CEPCI to longer roasting. CEPCI notes they have all the other characteristics of white kernels.
SWS and DW: scorched wholes seconds and dessert wholes
Whole kernels with more marks allowed, such as speckled, discoloured or shrivelled kernels.
B: butts
Kernels broken across (crosswise), but not split apart along their length.
S: splits
Kernels split along their length.
LWP, SWP and BB: large white pieces, small white pieces and baby bits
Broken kernels, sorted by sieve. LWP are kernels broken into more than two pieces. In the ISO standard, LWP stay on a 4.75 mm sieve, SWP on a 2.80 mm sieve and BB on a 1.70 mm sieve.
SB, SS, SP and SSP
Scorched butts, scorched splits, scorched pieces and scorched small pieces: the same cuts in the browner scorched colours.

The W210 to W500 rows are from AGMARK's rules, whose table starts at W210. The W180 row is from ISO 6477, the international standard for cashew kernels, and CEPCI lists W180 among the grades India exports.

Count it yourself. ISO 6477's test for the white whole grades counts the kernels in 1 kg or 1 lb. For a rough check at home, count the whole kernels in 100 g on a kitchen scale, multiply by 10 and compare with the per kg column.

AGMARK's whole grades allow up to 5% broken kernels at the time of packing.

What a grade tells you, and what it does not

  • Size. The number is a count, so W180 kernels are bigger than W320 kernels.
  • Colour. White wholes are white, pale ivory or light ash. Scorched grades are browner from scorching.
  • Whole or broken. Wholes, butts, splits and pieces are separate grades.
  • Limits on defects. AGMARK's white whole grades must be reasonably dry, free from insect damage, damaged kernels and black or brown spots, and completely free from testa, the kernel's skin. ISO 6477 caps moisture at 5%.

What a grade does not tell you. AGMARK's grade definitions are about count, colour, shape and defects, and none of them ranks flavour. So a W180 is a bigger kernel than a W320, not one graded as tastier. Scorched is a colour grade too: CEPCI notes scorched wholes have all the other characteristics of white kernels.

Which grade for which dish

  • To eat as they are, or toasted in a dry pan: whole kernels, in whatever size you like.
  • Jeedipappu pakodi: whole kernels, which stay crunchy inside the coating. Split any very large ones in half.
  • Jeedipappu upma: wholes or halves, fried golden in ghee and stirred in at the end.
  • Paramannam: our recipe halves the cashews before frying them in ghee, so splits do the same job.
  • Cooking, sweets and savoury snacks: butts, splits and pieces. CEPCI calls them ideal for these.
  • The everyday grade: CEPCI calls W320 the most popular grade and the most widely available worldwide.

Andhra's cashew belt

Andhra Pradesh is one of India's main cashew states. In 2017-18 it produced 14% of India's cashew nuts, second only to Maharashtra at 33%, going by CEPCI figures cited in a 2021 study.

In Srikakulam district alone, cashew grows on about 24,000 hectares, across the plains, the tribal high-altitude areas and the sea coast. Cashew kernels are the district's One District One Product for 2024.

Our cashew kernels come from the Chirala, Bapatla and Vizag coast and are packed in Narasaraopet.

Keeping cashews well

  • Keep the zip shut after use, or move the kernels to a dry, airtight jar.
  • Keep them in a cool, dry place, out of direct sunlight.
  • A rancid taste is taken as the first sign that nuts are going off, since most nuts hold a lot of oil. Taste one before you cook with a batch that has been open a while.
  • Use them within the best-before date on the pack.

Questions

What does W320 mean in cashew?

W320 means white whole cashew kernels at 300 to 320 kernels per pound, or 660 to 705 per kg, under AGMARK's rules. CEPCI calls it the most popular grade and the most widely available worldwide.

What is the difference between W240 and W320 cashews?

Size. W240 has 220 to 240 kernels per pound (485 to 530 per kg) and W320 has 300 to 320 (660 to 705 per kg), so W240 kernels are bigger. Both are white whole grades under AGMARK's rules.

Which cashew grade is best?

No grade is best for everything. W180 and W210 are the biggest whole kernels, W320 is the most widely available, and CEPCI calls butts, splits and pieces ideal for cooking and sweets. A grade describes size, colour and wholeness, not taste.

How many cashews are there in 1 kg?

It depends on the grade. Under AGMARK's rules, 1 kg of W240 holds 485 to 530 kernels, W320 holds 660 to 705 and W450 holds 880 to 990.

What does SW mean on cashews?

SW means scorched wholes: whole kernels with a slight brown colour from longer roasting. CEPCI notes they have all the other characteristics of white kernels.
