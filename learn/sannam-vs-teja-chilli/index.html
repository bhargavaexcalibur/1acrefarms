Sannam or Teja chilli powder: which to use

సన్నం కారం · తేజ కారం · सन्नम मिर्च पाउडर · तेजा मिर्च पाउडर · सन्नम-रक्तमरिचचूर्णम् · तेजा-रक्तमरिचचूर्णम् · Sannam Kaaram · Teja Kaaram

Sannam and Teja are two chilli types grown around Guntur and sold at the Guntur Mirchi Yard, Asia's largest dried red chilli market. Sannam is Telugu for thin, and the chilli is traded as the S4 type. Teja, also known as S17, is a hybrid.

Both go into curries, chutneys, podis and pickles. Of our two packs, Teja is the one marked extra hot. Here is what each one is, how heat and colour are measured, which to use where, and how to keep the powder. There is a full Telugu section further down.

In short

Sannam is Telugu for thin, and the chilli is traded as the S4 type; Teja (S17) is a hybrid. Both are grown around Guntur and sold at its Mirchi Yard. Use Sannam chilli powder for everyday curries, pulusu, pappu and tomato pachadi. Use Teja when you want a dish extra hot, as in kodi vepudu or gongura pachadi; our Teja pack is marked extra hot, so start with less. Keep both airtight, dry and out of the light.

What Sannam and Teja are

Sannam (S4)
Sannam is Telugu for thin, and Sannam chilli is traded as the S4 type. In a 2020 study of chillies from the Guntur Mirchi Yard, a variety called 334 is listed as Sannam S4. Spices Board's list of Indian chilli varieties has a Sannam S4 entry for chillies grown in Guntur, Warangal and Khammam districts, harvested from December to May.
Teja (S17)
A hybrid chilli, also known as S17. In Guntur district, hybrids such as Teja cover 60% of the chilli area, and open-pollinated varieties the other 40%, says the district's One District One Product page.
Where they are traded
The Guntur Mirchi Yard, about 5 km from the centre of Guntur, is known as Asia's biggest chilli market yard, and it influences chilli prices in India and abroad.

Heat and colour: how they are measured

Heat comes mainly from capsaicin and related compounds called capsaicinoids, and is measured in Scoville heat units (SHU).

Colour is measured in ASTA units, by a method of the American Spice Trade Association that measures the colour that can be extracted from the chilli. The higher the value, the brighter or richer the red. The red comes from pigments called carotenoids, such as capsanthin and capsorubin.

Sannam. Spices Board's entry for the Sannam S4 type grown in Guntur, Warangal and Khammam describes it as thick-skinned, hot and red, with an ASTA colour value of 32.11 and capsaicin of 0.226%.

Teja. Teja is not in that list. In a 2020 lab study of 19 chilli varieties collected at the Guntur Mirchi Yard, Teja (S-17) gave the second hottest concentrated extract of the 19, and a hotter one than 334 (Sannam S4).

We give no Scoville number for either chilli: that study measured extracts made from the chillies, not chilli powder, and Spices Board gives capsaicin as a percentage. Colour also changes with how the powder is packed and stored.

Which one to use

This is how our recipes use them:

  • Vegetable curries and pulusu: Sannam. Try gutti vankaya kura, kodi guddu pulusu or chepala pulusu.
  • Pappu and tomato pachadi: Sannam, as in tomato pappu and tomato pachadi.
  • Fry dishes: Teja, as in kodi vepudu.
  • Chicken, mutton and prawn curries: Teja, as in Andhra kodi kura, gongura mamsam and royyala iguru.
  • Gongura pachadi and podis: Teja, as in gongura pachadi and vellulli kaaram.
  • Snacks: Sannam, as in jeedipappu pakodi.
  • Pickles: either. Both packs list pickles among their uses.

Swapping and mixing

To use Teja where a recipe asks for Sannam, start with less, taste, and add more. Our Teja pack is marked extra hot. Going the other way, add Sannam a little at a time until the heat suits you.

To turn up the heat in a dish made with Sannam, mix in a little Teja.

Add either powder to hot oil on a low flame, so it does not burn.

Keeping it well

  • Keep the pack zipped shut, or move the powder to a dry, airtight jar. Air slowly breaks down the red pigments, so the colour fades.
  • Keep it out of the light, which speeds up that loss.
  • Keep it away from the stove. Warmth speeds it up too.
  • Use a dry spoon. Damp powder can grow mould.
  • Use it within the best-before date on the pack.

Questions

Which is hotter, Sannam or Teja chilli powder?

Of our two packs, Teja is the one marked extra hot. In a 2020 lab study of 19 chilli varieties from the Guntur Mirchi Yard, the extract from Teja (S-17) tested hotter than the one from 334 (Sannam S4). Start with a little and taste as you add.

What are S4 and S17 chillies?

S4 is the type Sannam chilli is traded as, and a variety called 334 is also listed as Sannam S4. S17 is another name for Teja, a hybrid chilli grown in Guntur district.

What does Sannam mean?

Sannam is Telugu for thin. Spices Board lists a Sannam S4 type grown in Guntur, Warangal and Khammam districts.

Can I use Teja chilli powder instead of Sannam?

Yes. Start with less Teja than the recipe asks for, taste, and add more. Going the other way, add Sannam a little at a time.

Why does chilli powder lose its colour?

Air slowly breaks down its red pigments, and light and warmth speed this up. Keep the pack zipped shut, out of the light and away from the stove.
